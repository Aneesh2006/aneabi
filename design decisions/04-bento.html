Direction 04 of 08

Bento

A tile for every part of your day together.

The idea

Bento treats a private app for two like a shared kitchen table covered in small, friendly boxes. Every feature gets its own soft colour and its own tile, so the home screen reads at a glance: Abi’s love note, Abi’s corner, Water, Goa. The dashboard and the giant widget are cut from the same tiles, which makes the phone feel like one place whether the app is open or not.

ModularWarmBrightTactileCalm
How it feels

Tiles squish to 97% under a thumb and spring back in about a quarter of a second. Numbers roll like an odometer when a counter moves. A love note lands with a soft bounce and a two-beat haptic, +1 gives one light tick, and Done gives a firmer click while the tile settles to a quieter colour.

Dark mode

Dark is a translation, not an inversion. The canvas is #101010, not black. Tiles go deep and quiet (the same five hues, darker and warmer), accents and person colours get brighter, the focus tile turns bone, and the love note stays coral so it still glows. Same layout, same content, same tokens with a second set of values.

Design system

Everything the build needs, in the names the code will use. Light and dark values come from the same token list; components below are the real ones the screens above are made of.

Colour

Five soft hues carry the features and one ink carries every word. People get a fixed colour each (Ane blue, Abi coral). Person colours are fills, dots, rings and tints, never text: ink sits on them (4.8:1 on blue, 6.5:1 on coral). Red is for overdue and destructive things only.

Rules
  • Text is ink on every fill. Person colours are never text colours.
  • Body is 15 px; nothing under 11 px, and captions stay 12 px or more.
  • Numerals that change (counters, times) use tabular figures so they do not jitter while they roll.
  • Emoji come from Noto Color Emoji so they look like Android’s.
  • Layouts survive large text: tiles grow in height, rows never clip critical words.

Shape, space and elevation

One module rules the screen: four columns of 86 dp with 12 dp gutters (16 dp margins), so every tile is 1, 2 or 4 columns wide and 1, 2 or 3 rows high. Radii are generous and few. Tiles are flat: colour does the separating, so only things that float (the dock, sheets, the quick-note card) get a shadow.

Motion

Motion is small and physical. Tiles squish when pressed and spring back. Numbers roll instead of swapping. Three moments get a signature. Everything respects the system’s reduce-motion setting: the squish and roll become instant cross-fades, haptics stay.

Widget specification
  • Tile: Box + background(ColorProvider) + cornerRadius(20.dp), 12 dp padding, 8 dp between tiles, 16 dp from the page edge.
  • Two-up rows: Row of tiles with defaultWeight(); the status tile gets a larger weight.
  • Text: system font only. Title 15–20 / Medium, body 14–15 / Regular, caption 12, counter numerals 38–52 / Bold (Glance has no letter-spacing).
  • Pips: eight small Boxes, 6 dp high, filled ink or track colour.
  • Icons: Phosphor Bold exported as vector drawables, tinted by ColorFilter.
  • Handwriting and avatars: bitmaps rendered from the stored strokes (transparent background, ink or bone pen).
  • Buttons: 40 dp drawn, 48 dp tappable (GlanceModifier.size(48.dp) around a 40 dp box).
Behaviour
  • The column scrolls; the quick-action bar does not. A medium size drops the bento to one compact list tile plus the bar.
  • Taps (Done, Snooze, +1, tick) update the tile at once and sync in the background; the widget shows “updated 14 min ago” in its corner when data is older than 15 minutes.
  • Tapping a love note, the scratchpad or a message opens the quick-note editor or chat at the right place; the full app never has to open.
  • Each placed widget chooses its sections; hidden sections simply collapse.
05

Notes for the build

What is stock, what is custom, what could bite, how the widget keeps the look inside Glance, and what keeps it accessible. Everything above is drawn to be built as it stands.

Flutter: stock
  • Framework parts, themed not rebuilt: Scaffold, CustomScrollView with slivers, TextField, Switch, SegmentedButton (ink selected, white track), SnackBar (floating, with an Undo action), showModalBottomSheet for the map sheet and pickers, ListTile for Settings rows.
  • Packages: google_fonts (bundle Bricolage Grotesque and DM Sans as assets so the app is fully offline), phosphor_flutter (Bold and Fill), flutter_staggered_grid_view for the 4-column module, and one map package with a custom pastel style (flutter_map or MapLibre).
  • Theme: a BentoColors ThemeExtension holds every token from the Design system, light and dark, plus BentoType for the ten text styles. No hex values in widgets, so dark mode stays a translation.
Flutter: custom, written once
  • BentoTile: hue, padding 16, radius 24, optional press squish (AnimatedScale to 0.97 in 120 ms, release 280 ms with an out-back curve). BentoGrid wraps the 4-column module. TileHead is the disc, label and open arrow.
  • Pips (water, backup, milestone), RollingNumber (per-digit slide with tabular figures), FloatingDock (the active item morphs into a hue pill), AvatarPair, MoodPicker, LoveNoteTile (arrival animation and the ghost heart).
  • HandwritingCanvas: a CustomPainter over a stroke model (points, width, ink, round caps). The same model drives the Compose quick-note editor and the Glance bitmap renderer, so a note drawn anywhere looks the same everywhere.
  • WaveformBars, the stylised map layer (pins, accuracy circle, saved-place markers) and the sticker renderer.
Risky to build
  • Squish on every tile while scrolling. Keep each tile in a RepaintBoundary, scale only, no opacity layers, and no blur anywhere in scrolling content (the quick-note scrim is the only dimming). The dashboard has about 40 tiles; build the part below the fold lazily and cache photos at twice their tile size.
  • Rolling numerals need tabular figures or the digits jitter. Under reduce-motion, fall back to a cross-fade.
  • Handwriting latency. Batch pointer samples, extend the current path incrementally, repaint only the canvas. Budget a native fallback if the Flutter canvas feels slower than the Compose one.
  • Three renderers, one look. The quick-note editor and the alarm are native Compose, so Bricolage and DM Sans also ship as Android font resources and the tile shapes are Compose Surfaces. Generate a Kotlin colour file from the token list so the two worlds cannot drift.
  • The map. Without tiles it needs a styled vector source or a custom painter; the accuracy circle must convert metres to pixels at every zoom, and saved places must work offline.
  • Emoji. The board uses Noto Color Emoji; devices use their own. Check that 28 px mood emoji and the 🍝 in titles do not clip on OEM fonts.
  • Contrast on photos. Photo tiles always carry a solid label tile; do not replace it with text over the picture.
Widgets: how the look survives Glance
  • No blur, glass or shadow is needed. Bento is flat colour, so the widget matches the app. The widget background is transparent and the tiles float on the wallpaper; each tile is a Box with a ColorProvider(day, night) fill and cornerRadius(20.dp) (API 31+, which is the minimum).
  • System font only. Glance cannot load fonts and has no letter-spacing, so counter numerals rely on size and Bold alone and look slightly looser than in the app. Roboto is about 8% narrower than DM Sans: leave that slack in every row.
  • Structure: a root Column holding a LazyColumn (weight 1) and the fixed quick-action bar. Two-up rows are Rows of defaultWeight() boxes; keep nesting shallow so RemoteViews stay under the size limit.
  • Pips, icons, handwriting: eight small Boxes for pips; Phosphor glyphs as vector drawables; strokes and avatars as bitmaps. The scratchpad tile can be rendered as one bitmap (text and strokes together) so the doodle always lines up with the words, at the price of selectable text.
  • Actions: Done, Snooze, +1 and list ticks use actionRunCallback, update the tile optimistically, then sync. Buttons are 40 dp drawn with a 48 dp hit box.
  • Sizes: SizeMode.Responsive switches between the compact list (medium), the bento (large) and the full column (page). Discreet mode is a separate layout of names and counts, so no content is ever composed for it.
  • Colour source: fixed brand colours, light or dark by the system theme; not Material You, because the hues carry meaning.
Accessibility
  • Contrast: ink is the only text colour on any fill; every text pair on the board is 4.5:1 or better (table in the colour section). Person colours are never text.
  • Targets: 48 dp minimum. The check circle (40) and +1 button (40 high) have a 48 dp hit box; list rows are at least 48 dp high.
  • Text size: tiles size to their content, so at 130% and 200% a 2×1 tile grows to a 2×2. Rows use minimum heights, never fixed ones, and no critical word is truncated. Body is 15 px, nothing under 11 px.
  • Semantics: one merged node per tile with a spoken summary (“Call mom, overdue 1 hour 15 minutes”) and custom actions for Done, Snooze and +1. Counters announce the result (“Water, 6 of 8”). The dock is a navigation landmark.
  • Colour is never alone: overdue is an icon, a word and a chip; assignees are a dot plus a word; done is a tick plus a quieter tile; Ane’s and Abi’s bubbles also differ by side.
  • Motion and touch feedback: reduce-motion turns the squish and the roll into cross-fades; haptics are optional in Settings; nothing flashes.
  • Handwriting: always offer typing as the other route, and give a handwriting block a spoken label (“Handwritten note”) since it is not searchable.
Open decisions to confirm with the owners
  • The dock holds Home, Chat, Reminders, Gallery and Together. Notes, Lists, Calendar, Wishlists, Countdowns, Map, Wrapped and Settings sit in the “Our spaces” grid on Home and inside Together. If Notes should be on the dock, Together moves into the grid.
  • Coral for the love-note tile is the loudest colour in the app on purpose. If it feels too loud in a dark room, the dark value can drop to #E9684A.
  • The overdue tile uses the focus colour (ink or bone). The alternative is a red-tinted tile; it reads calmer but loses the single clear focal point per screen.
