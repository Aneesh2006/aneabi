Paper & Ink

A shared journal, kept in two inks.

WarmLiteraryQuietHandwrittenTimeless

Concept

The app becomes the notebook the two of you keep together. Warm paper, ink-black type and a single vermilion pencil give every screen the calm of a well-kept journal, so handwriting, love notes and doodles look native instead of bolted on. Ane writes in ink blue and Abi in vermilion, so one glance tells you whose hand it is.

How it feels

Soft fades and gentle settles, 140–420 ms, never bouncy. Marking something done draws an ink tick with a small pen flick, +1 adds a stroke to the tally, and a love note arrives like a folded note opening. Haptics stay light: one soft tick for a tap, a gentle double tick when a love note lands.

01 · Home screen

On the home screen

The giant widget is the notebook’s cover: today’s page, the love note, Abi’s corner and the shared scratchpad, readable without opening the app. Widgets use the phone’s system fonts (Noto Serif for section heads, Roboto for text) and fixed brand colours, so they stay inside what Glance can draw. The editor that rises over the home screen is native Compose and may use the full type system.

02 · In the app

In the app

Section heads sit under a thin newspaper rule, slips of raised paper carry what you act on, and hairlines carry everything else. Ane’s things are ink blue, Abi’s are vermilion, and the vermilion fill is reserved for the one primary action on a screen.

Reading the dashboard

One long page, ordered by what needs you first. Every block either acts in place or opens its own screen, and nothing is there only for decoration. Sections are separated by a thin ink rule; slips of raised paper mark the few things you act on.

  1. Masthead. “Ane & Abi” in the two inks, today’s date, Settings, and the running day count with the next milestone. Tapping the count opens Us.
  2. Love note. Pinned until dismissed. Reply opens the quick-note editor in love-note mode; Dismiss shows an Undo snackbar and tells Abi it was seen. The seal and creases are the only ornament on the page.
  3. Abi’s corner. Status with its “until” time, mood with the one-line note, saved place, and Abi’s reminder progress. The slip below opens Chat at the first unread message.
  4. Mood check-in. Shown until you answer. One tap saves; a second tap adds a line.
  5. Today. A planner page in time order. Tick, +1, Done and Snooze all work in place, and the “Now” ribbon splits what is behind you from what is next. Overdue gets a madder margin rule, never a banner.
  6. Scratchpad. Ruled paper, edited in place, with Abi’s doodle arriving live.
  7. Countdown, On this day, plans, photos. Prints with captions rather than cards; “don’t show again” sits in the memory’s overflow.
  8. Contents. The eight destinations that are not in the bottom bar. With Chat, Gallery, Reminders and Us in the bar, and Settings also on the masthead, all eleven destinations are one tap away.

Height. About 2,500 px at 412 wide, built from a single column so it translates to a Flutter CustomScrollView of slivers with no nested scrolling.

The alarm is a different room

This screen is native Compose, shown over the lock screen by a full-screen intent. It ignores the app theme on purpose: it is always night paper, because it fires at 6 AM and 9 PM in dark rooms and has to read over any wallpaper.

  1. One job. Vitamins, in the largest serif on any screen, then the context that matters: the schedule, the 34-day streak and that Abi has already done theirs.
  2. Done is the biggest target (76 dp tall, full width) and sits in the thumb zone. Snooze is a single tap on 5, 10, 30 or 60 minutes, with 10 pre-selected.
  3. The seal rings. Two concentric rings expand slowly from the vermilion seal (a 4 s loop). With reduced motion they stay still.
  4. Sound. Alarm stream, so it rings through silent mode and Do Not Disturb, until Done or Snooze. The phone-health check warns when the full-screen permission is off.

Fonts are bundled in the APK for this screen (Fraunces and Instrument Sans), which native Compose allows. The seal and rings are a small Canvas drawing, not an image.

03 · Dark mode

Night paper, same notebook

The system translates rather than inverts. Paper becomes warm charcoal, ink becomes ivory, shadows give way to hairlines and a slightly lighter surface, vermilion lifts to a coral that still carries a dark label at 5.8:1, and the two person inks lighten while keeping their hue.

What changes in the dark

The roles stay the same, the values are re-chosen. Contrast is checked per pair, not assumed from the light theme.

  2. Fills flip their label. On the lifted vermilion the label turns charcoal, 5.8:1, instead of ivory.
  3. Photos dim 8% and the ruled-paper lines drop to a whisper so text, not texture, leads.

Rules of use

  • Nothing under 12 px; body text is 14 px or more; labels are the only uppercase.
  • Layouts survive 200% text: rows grow, nothing is clipped, chips wrap.
  • Curly quotes and apostrophes, en dashes in ranges (9 AM–9 PM), × for multiples.
  • One serif size per screen is the hero: the love note, the days, the alarm’s task.

Built with Glance, no tricks

  • Colours are a ColorProvider(day, night) pair taken from the table; no dynamic colour.
  • Type: FontFamily.Serif for section heads at 15 sp / 600, SansSerif for the rest; no custom fonts.
  • Shape: 26 dp outer radius (the Android 12 widget radius), 8–10 dp inside; sections are separated by 1 dp dividers, not nested cards.
  • Ink, tally, ruled paper, handwriting are bitmaps the app renders at the widget’s pixel size; the tick and icons are vector drawables.
  • Layout: a LazyColumn of sections with the quick-action Row fixed beneath it, outside the scroll.
  • Discreet mode swaps each section’s content for its name and a count; the action bar is unchanged.
05 · Notes for the build

Notes for the build

What is stock, what is custom, what could bite, how the widget keeps the look inside Glance’s limits, and what accessibility has to hold. The design is built exactly as drawn; anything that needs a decision is flagged here instead of improvised.

Stock vs custom in Flutter

Stock use as is
  • Scaffold, CustomScrollView + slivers (the dashboard is one long sliver list)
  • TextField, Switch, SnackBar, showModalBottomSheet / DraggableScrollableSheet (map sheet), Dialog
  • Image caching and grids for the gallery (SliverGrid, thumbnails from the media pipeline)
  • System emoji on Android; Hero and implicit animations for fades and settles
Themed stock widget, our tokens
  • One ThemeExtension<PaperTokens> holding every colour in 4.1, plus ThemeData for light and dark
  • TextTheme from 4.2 (Fraunces + Instrument Sans) with tabular-figure features on counters
  • FilledButton (vermilion), OutlinedButton (ink), TextButton, Chip for assignee and status chips, SegmentedButton for Mine / Abi’s / All
  • NavigationBar for the five tabs; the vermilion bookmark ribbon is a small painter on the selected item
Custom drawn with CustomPainter
  • Ink tick and ring (animated PathMetric), tally marks, goal arc, the “now” ribbon
  • Wax seal shape, love-note creases, the double rule, ruled paper, the die-cut sticker border
  • Handwriting canvas, stroke model and renderer (shared algorithm with Kotlin, see risks)
  • Map from vector style data: blocks, hatched water, stippled park, pins, accuracy circle
  • Voice waveform bars, mood ring draw-on, avatar monograms

Risky to build ranked

  • High
    One stroke algorithm, three renderers. Handwriting is drawn in Flutter (notes, chat), in the native Compose quick-note editor, and as bitmaps for widgets. Define the smoothing (Catmull-Rom to cubic, tension 0.5, round caps, width by pen) once, store strokes as plain point lists, and port it with shared test vectors, as with the crypto. Strokes are append-only, so merging live edits is simple and the stroke eraser is a tombstone.
  • High
    Fraunces axes in Flutter. The google_fonts package fetches static instances, so opsz, SOFT and WONK need the variable TTF bundled as an asset (still from Google Fonts) and set through fontVariations. If that fails on a device, the fallback is static Fraunces at SOFT 0 with opsz 72 for display sizes; layouts are drawn with enough slack for either.
  • Medium
    The map is a style, not a tile set. The look (tan blocks, cream streets, hatched water, stippled parks) needs a vector source and a custom painter. If the data source is added later, the hatching and stipple can fall back to flat fills without breaking the layout.
  • Medium
    Dashboard length. About 2,500 px of mixed content. Keep it a single sliver list, lazy-build below the fold, and draw ruled paper and creases with painters rather than image assets.
  • Medium
    Tabular figures. Checked in the served fonts: Instrument Sans has tabular figures; Fraunces does not, so its numerals are proportional. The design therefore keeps Fraunces numerals for hero numbers (days, countdowns, the 5/8 headline) and uses Instrument Sans FontFeature.tabularFigures() for anything that ticks in a row. Re-check this in the TTFs you bundle.
  • Low
    Love-note unfolding. The 3-D flap is a nice-to-have; the system degrades to a 240 ms fade plus the seal pop, and reduced motion shows the final frame.
  • Low
    No backdrop blur anywhere. Nothing in this direction needs it, so there is no scroll-performance risk. The quick-note editor may use window blur on phones that have it; the scrim in 4.1 is the designed fallback.

Widgets inside Glance’s limits

  • Fixed brand colours as a day/night pair; Material You is not used, so paper stays paper on any wallpaper.
  • System fonts only: serif for section heads, sans for the rest. Avatars, handwriting and the scratchpad paper are bitmaps.
  • No blur, glass or shadows. Separation from the wallpaper is the surface fill, plus a 1 dp lighter edge drawn as an inset Box where the wallpaper is near-white.
  • Giant widget = LazyColumn of sections with the quick-action Row fixed below it. Rows are 44 dp tall with the whole row tappable.
  • Every tick, +1 and Done fires a broadcast and updates the widget immediately, then syncs.

Accessibility

  • Contrast is verified per pair in 4.1: body 15:1, muted 5.3:1, accent text 5.3:1, label on vermilion 4.6:1 (5.8:1 in dark). Large text only where 3:1 would be enough.
  • Touch targets are 48 dp or more. Buttons are 48 dp tall; segments and text links are drawn smaller but sit in a 48 dp hit area; chips and rows are tappable across their full width.
  • Colour is never alone. Monograms read An and Ab, chips carry names, overdue says “Overdue” beside a clock, a tick sits beside its row’s state words, locks sit beside “Only you can see these”.
  • Text scaling to 200%: rows grow, chips wrap, the planner’s time column widens, nothing is clipped. Widgets follow the system font scale and scroll.
  • Screen readers. A reminder row is one stop: “Gym, done at 7:12 AM, streak 9”. +1, Done and Snooze are custom actions. The love note reads “Love note from Abi, 5:58 PM” and then its text.
  • Handwriting has no text alternative (it is not searchable by design); it is announced as “Handwriting by Ane” with its position in the note.
  • Motion and haptics. Reduced motion shows final frames; haptics can be switched off. The alarm’s seal rings are decorative and silent.
  • Dark and alarm. Dark pairs are checked separately; the alarm is always dark, with a 76 dp Done target and one-tap snooze.

Decisions to confirm with the owners

  • Vermilion is #CF4125, not #D9462B. Slightly deeper so every primary label passes 4.5:1. The original survives nowhere; say if you would rather keep it and use a charcoal label.
  • Abi’s ink and the action colour are the same vermilion. The system keeps them apart by form (Abi is a mark, a dot or a tint; the action is the single solid fill) and by madder for overdue. If this still reads ambiguous, the alternative is ink-black primary buttons with vermilion left for Abi and the seal.
  • Bottom bar: Home, Chat, Gallery, Reminders, Us. Notes live under the scratchpad’s “Notes” link and in Contents; say if Notes should take a tab and Reminders move into Us.
  • The alarm is always night paper, independent of the theme setting.
