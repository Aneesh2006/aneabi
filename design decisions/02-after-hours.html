After Hours

Texting under the covers: lights low, one warm glow each, only the two of you.

  • Intimate
  • Candlelit
  • Hushed
  • Tender
  • Unhurried

This is the app at the hour you actually use it: phone in hand, room dark, the person you love one message away. Deep aubergine surfaces keep the screen quiet and cheap on OLED, and light is rationed. Amber is always Ane, rose is always Abi, and a soft glow appears only where something tender happens, a love note landing or the day count turning over. Everything else is large-radius, calm and in no hurry, so the app never shouts in a dark room.

How it feels Motion eases out slowly and never bounces. The love note’s halo breathes on a five-second loop, the water counter fills like lamps being lit one by one, and ticking a reminder draws a mint ring. Haptics stay soft: one light tick for +1, a gentle double tap when a love note lands, a firm confirm for Done.

01 · Widgets

On the home screen

The app is useful without being opened. The giant widget carries the whole evening in one column: Abi’s love note, Abi’s corner, your reminders and the shared scratchpad, with four actions pinned underneath that never scroll away. Opaque aubergine, amber and rose accents, Roboto: nothing a launcher can’t draw.

02 · Screens

In the app

Eight screens, one language: big radii, tonal layers instead of borders, and light only where people are. Amber marks Ane and rose marks Abi everywhere, from the avatar to the bubble edge to the map pin. The brightest control on a screen is the one to press.

How the dashboard is built

One scroll, ordered by what matters at 7:15 on an evening. Every block is either actionable in place or opens its screen.

03 · Light mode

Dawn, the light counterpart

After Hours is dark-first, so its light theme is a translation, not an inversion. Surfaces turn lilac-white and gain soft plum shadows, the glows become warm washes of the same two colours, the primary button flips to plum, and Ane’s amber and Abi’s rose get deeper text variants so they still pass contrast on white. Layout, type and shape do not move at all.

What changes, what stays

Everything below is a token swap in [data-theme="light"]. No component is redrawn.

05 · Handoff

Notes for the build

What to take off the shelf, what to draw by hand, what could bite, how the widgets keep the look inside the limits of Glance, and the accessibility rules that are part of the design rather than a pass at the end.

widgetsStock versus custom in Flutter

Most of the app is stock Material 3, themed from the tokens. The distinctive parts are a short list of custom widgets, and most of those are a gradient, a shape and an animation controller.

Stock, themed

  • Navigation and chrome: NavigationBar with a filled selectedIcon and a --sel indicator, AppBar, bottom sheets with a 28 dp top radius, dialogs.
  • Controls: FilledButton (moon), FilledButton.tonal, OutlinedButton, TextButton, IconButton, SegmentedButton, Switch, TextField with a 1.5 dp --outline border, floating SnackBar.
  • Chips: Chip and FilterChip with pill shape and the person tints for You, Abi and Both.
  • Theme: a hand-written ColorScheme.dark and ColorScheme.light from the token table (primary is the moon, error is the red), plus one ThemeExtension for the person colours, glows, bubble tints and the “moment” gradients. No seed-colour generation.
  • Packages: google_fonts with Sora and Manrope bundled as assets (no runtime download), material_symbols_icons (Rounded, weight 300), flutter_svg for the sticker and empty-state art.

Custom, built once and reused

  • Orb avatar with the presence ring: a radial gradient in a circle, a ring, a dot.
  • Lamp counter: a row of eight AnimatedContainers. Lit lamps get a soft glow in the app and none in the widget.
  • Love-note card: two radial-gradient blobs in a clipped Stack and one looping AnimationController (5.2 s). The same card is used on the dashboard and in the chat.
  • Chat: bubble shape and tint with a 1 px edge in the sender’s colour, quote strip, hanging reaction chip, voice-note waveform from stored peaks (CustomPainter), typing dots.
  • Handwriting: the canvas, the stroke model, pen widths, four inks, stroke eraser, undo and redo. Strokes render to a transparent PNG for widgets.
  • Together ring (sweep gradient painter), map (painter in the same palette), live cursors with name flags, gallery grid with who-added dots.
  • Native Compose: the quick-note editor and the alarm screen, using the same tokens (see Handoff).

warningRisky or fiddly to build

Ordered by how likely each is to cost real time.

  • Handwriting latency and storage. It has to feel instant. Draw incrementally in a RepaintBoundary, keep strokes as immutable items so merging stays simple, and decide the stroke format with the sync design before building the eraser.
  • Live cursors with name flags. Flutter has no remote-caret API for EditableText. Plan a custom text layout or a CRDT position map; budget it as its own task.
  • Glow and scroll performance. Use RadialGradient in a DecoratedBox, not big blurred shadows. One RepaintBoundary per glow, pause the loop when off screen, at most two animated glows on a screen. No BackdropFilter over scrolling content.
  • Dawn contrast. The amber and rose fills fail as text on white. Always use ane-text and abi-text for text, and add a lint or a test that rejects the fill tokens in TextStyle.
  • Tokens across three toolkits. Flutter, Compose and Glance cannot see each other’s theme. Generate Dart and Kotlin token files from one JSON, the same way this board generates its tables.
  • The map. Drawn here in SVG. In the app it is either a custom painter (no network, exact palette) or a map SDK with a style built from the tokens. Either way the ±12 m ring must scale with zoom, from metres to pixels.
  • Emoji metrics. Sora and Manrope with Android’s emoji can nudge line height. Set a fixed strut and check rows that mix text and emoji.
  • The alarm. Reliability beats beauty: full-screen intent, alarm channel, and nothing animated may ever delay the Done button.

accessibility_newAccessibility

Built into the tokens and components, so screens inherit it.

  • Contrast. Every text pair in the table under Colour passes 4.5:1 in both themes; muted text is never below 4.8:1; input and switch boundaries use --outline at 3:1 or better.
  • Touch targets. Every control has a 48 × 48 dp hit area, even when it draws at 36 or 40 (MaterialTapTargetSize.padded, and explicit hit slop in Glance).
  • Never colour alone. People are a monogram, a name and a colour. Done is a check and a word, overdue is an icon and “Overdue 1 h 15 min”, lamps come with “5/8”.
  • Large text. Rows use minimum heights, chips wrap, text is flexible. Test at 200% font scale; widgets use sp and clip gracefully.
  • Motion. Honour Reduce Motion: halos stop breathing, lamps and ticks appear instantly. Loops are slow (5.2 s) and soft, nothing flashes.
  • Screen readers. The love note reads “Love note from Abi, 5:58 PM, Proud of you for today…”. The counter reads “Water, 5 of 8 glasses”. Handwriting blocks are announced as “Handwriting block” with an optional text alternative. Snooze lengths are separate buttons.
  • Haptics reinforce an action and are never the only signal.

Copy these as they are

  • Colour, radius, spacing, type: the CSS custom properties on :root and [data-theme="light"]. Names map to AfterHoursColors.surface2, .aneText and so on.
  • Motion: Cubic(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) for ease-soft, Cubic(0.45, 0, 0.2, 1) for ease in-out, durations 120, 220, 360, 600 ms and 5.2 s for the breathing loop.
  • Haptics: lightImpact for +1, mediumImpact for Done, a short two-pulse pattern for a love note arriving.
